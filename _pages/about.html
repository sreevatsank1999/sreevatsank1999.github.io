I'm a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at UC San Diego. I build networks and systems for Physical AI: robots that sense the world, offload their thinking to the edge, and act over wireless links.

I work with Prof. Dinesh Bharadia in the WCSNG lab, and my background is in wireless systems. Before Physical AI, I architected Nomad, a 40 Gbit/s spectrum sensing system built for an IARPA program, and worked on PHY-layer datasets and machine learning for signal detection. I hold an M.S. from UC San Diego and a B.Tech. in Electrical Engineering from IIT Indore. Along the way I have interned at Qualcomm, JASR Systems, and Télécom Paris, and spent a year as a DevOps engineer at Tata Digital.

Research Overview

Robots increasingly offload perception and policy inference, such as vision-language-action models, to GPUs at the network edge. That puts the wireless network inside the robot's control loop. Every camera, LiDAR, and IMU stream for a control step has to reach the edge, together and on time, before the next action can be computed. Yet today's networks treat those streams as unrelated flows, and today's sensing stacks ignore the network. My research closes this gap from both sides. I quantify what Physical AI applications actually need from the network, design RAN mechanisms that deliver multimodal sensor data in lock-step, and build testbeds that expose the full path from sensing to action.
